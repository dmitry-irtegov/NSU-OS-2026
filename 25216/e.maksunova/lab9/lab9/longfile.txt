That's what you have done, it's working. 
You end up with two processes, and both the parent and child are printing at the same time 
(likely on two different cores too) so it gets mixed together weirdly like that.

You're creating pipes, which would allow you to communicate with the parent, but you're 
not overwriting stdin/stdout, so those still go to the same stdin/stdout as the parent 
as part of the same process group. 
When you fork, you inherit all the file descriptors too, that's why you open the pipes before forking, 
and then the child inherit those and you can use one side in the child and the other in the parent.

If you want to redirect stdout to the parent, 
you probably want to dup2(p1[0], 0) and dup2(p2[1], 1) and then 
you can use p1[1] to send stuff to the child's stdin and p2[0] to read from the child's stdout.
